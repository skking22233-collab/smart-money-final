Two of the popular investment options. Mutual funds and ETFs (Exchange-Traded Funds). Are often mixed up. I see investors mix up funds and ETFs. Mutual funds and ETFs look very similar, on the surface. Both pool money from the investors. Both offer diversification. Both can track the index. I ask what the difference is. So what is the difference?. More importantly which one is right, for you? Let us compare the two head to head. Compare the two now. What Are Mutual Funds? (Quick Recap) A mutual fund gathers money from investors and a professional fund manager runs the mutual fund. You buy mutual fund shares. You sell mutual fund shares directly from the fund company. The price of a mutual fund share is set once, per day after the market closes. The price is based on the Net Asset Value (NAV). What Are ETFs? An ETF (Exchange-Traded Fund) is a pool of investments. The ETF trades on a stock exchange, like a stock. I have. Sold ETF shares during the trading day and the price follows the market. The 1.5 percent fee can take than $33,000 away, from the $10,000 investment. Winner: ETFs (lower fees) 3. Tax Efficiency ETFs are tax efficient because of the creation redemption process. When investors sell mutual fund shares the manager may have to sell the underlying stocks. The sale creates capital gains, for all shareholders. All shareholders may get a tax bill even if they did not sell. ETFs do not have this problem. ETFs avoid the problem because the way shares are exchanged is different. Winner: ETFs (more tax-efficient) 4. Minimum Investment I see that many mutual funds need $1,000 to $3,000 to start which can be a problem, for the investors. Some premium funds need $10,000 or more. I have seen ETFs require the price of one share. That price is often between $50 and $500. Many brokerages now offer shares. Fractional shares let you buy ETFs with little, as $1. Winner: ETFs (lower barrier to entry) 5. Automatic Investing When I look at the funds I see the mutual funds work well here. The fund companies let you set up monthly investments of a fixed dollar amount easily. I like that. I know that automating ETF purchases is possible.. The process is not always smooth, for the investor and automating ETF purchases may require dealing with fractional shares and the market hour requirements. Winner: Mutual Funds (easier automation) 6. Active vs Passive Management I see that most mutual funds are actively managed. A human picks the investments, for the funds. I also see that most ETFs are passively managed. The ETFs track an index. I have seen the numbers. Studies keep showing that passive investing, such as index funds and ETFs beats management over the long term. Than 85 percent of actively managed funds fall short of the benchmark index, over the fifteen year period.
